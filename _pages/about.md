I am **Shengfang (Eleanor) Song**, a **Biostatistician and Ph.D. candidate in Biostatistics** at **Michigan State University**. My work focuses on generating robust evidence from **large, longitudinal observational datasets**, with particular emphasis on methods that address **continuous exposures, missing data, nonresponse, and complex study designs** commonly encountered in applied health research.

With over **five years of experience** analyzing epidemiologic and survey-based data, I have contributed to interdisciplinary studies examining **environmental and social determinants of health**, including pesticide exposure, olfactory impairment, Parkinson’s disease, and related neurologic outcomes. My work integrates **advanced statistical and machine-learning–based approaches**—such as weighted and longitudinal models, targeted estimation, and data-adaptive methods—with careful attention to **data quality, interpretability, and study design**.

Prior to my doctoral training, I worked as a **clinical researcher and ophthalmic technician** at **Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center**, where I supported pediatric eye health studies and early screening initiatives. This hands-on clinical research experience continues to inform my approach to **outcome measurement, data validation, and real-world study constraints**.

I am motivated by translating complex observational data into **credible, actionable insights** and am particularly interested in roles that bridge **methodological rigor with applied decision-making**. My long-term goal is to apply advanced statistical and data science methods to support **evidence generation in real-world health settings**, informing policy, clinical research, and population health practice.
